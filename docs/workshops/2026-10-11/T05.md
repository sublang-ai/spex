## Day 2: procedures

## What happened

- **Day 2.** `design` was described in chat: an architect drafts a decision record with an options table and spec items, a client advocate reviews it in up to two rounds, the account lead signs off, one commit on approval and nothing committed on rejection. Three compiles were needed: a two-word role ("Client Advocate", W-12), then the linker refused drafting steps that edit files without committing (W-10) — the agent moved the drafts into the architect's replies, which changed the team's design. Registration bound Architect to `dev.coder` and Advocate to `dev.reviewer` (both GPT-6.1 Sol, xhigh).
- **Day 3.** `tripsplit-core` was created from the palette and paired into the team's subgroup. `/design` on the Wanderly brief first asked the account lead two questions (settlement currency, where rates come from) with three assumptions; answered, the architect drafted, the advocate asked for fixes over two rounds, and the sign-off question came back to the account lead with two choices to confirm. The first approval was taken as "the boss's" and the playbook asked the account lead again: the authored text names the account lead as a person apart from the Boss, so the Captain relays the same question twice. Approved a second time, the architect found it had already committed the design in the earlier turn (`dfe4e73`, DR-001 plus the `trip-split` package) and asked about a missing reviewer credit; told to keep the commit, the final step failed the run because it "committed nothing" (W-25). Dropped; the design stands.
- **Sharing.** `design` was published to the workshop org (after the org and license edits, W-18/W-21) and T08 added it from the registry and enabled it with its own players.

## What they found

- W-10: the most natural authoring shape — draft files in the tree, commit at the end — cannot link; the workaround changes what the team wanted.
- W-12: the two-word role cost a compile round.
- Authoring lesson: naming the approver as someone other than "the Boss" in the playbook text makes the runtime ask twice; the course should say the Boss is whoever answers.
