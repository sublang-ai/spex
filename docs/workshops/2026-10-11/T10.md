# T10 — Atlas Platform

## Who they are

The platform and SRE team of a 150-person engineering organization; Go; GitOps, trunk-based, canary rollouts.

## Day 2: procedures

`rollout` (deploy): canary stages, health signals, rollbacks, a dry run, reviewed by an SRE, then the owner's go/no-go question; `incident` (plan). Adopts T01's `shape`.

## Day 3: project

`rollout-check`: a canary rule checker CLI, created in Spex.

## What happened

- **Day 2.** `rollout` was described in chat: a release engineer writes a canary plan for one change, dry-runs the deploy, an SRE reviews in up to two rounds, then the owner is asked whether to start. The compile passed after one rename (the two-word role, W-12); registration bound ReleaseEngineer to `dev.coder` and SRE to `dev.reviewer`.
- **Day 3.** `rollout-check` was created from the palette and paired into the team's subgroup. The built-in `/code` built the Go CLI in three commits over three review rounds. `/rollout` first asked where the deploy manifests and script were (the repository had none); told to define the layout itself, it wrote the plan, the config and the deploy script (`763c8ca`), dry-ran the first stage, and the SRE's two review rounds ended with one finding standing — the false-negative rule has no real old-script results to replay against — so the run ended as "not cleared to start", exactly as the team's procedure says it should. T01's `shape` was added from the registry for the plan's second stage.
- **Sharing.** `rollout` was published to the workshop org and T03 added it from the registry. After the Playbook upgrade, `rollout` was recompiled and republished as 0.1.1 — the course's "update your version" step — and T03 received it through its `^0.1.0` request on the next resolve.
- **Incident.** `incident` was described in chat: a responder writes an incident record (timeline, impact, root-cause hypothesis with the code paths) and an intent record with acceptance checks, commits both, and a lead reviews in up to two rounds. The agent asked one question (findings left after round two: stop, not cleared) and compiled on the first attempt in nine minutes; Responder → `dev.coder`, Lead → `dev.reviewer`; published as `claude-workshop-261011/incident@0.1.0` (package commit `2f73ec0`). `/incident` on the on-call's report wrote `docs/incidents/2026-10-11-payments-api-canary-percent-threshold.md` and IR-001 (`851fefd`), showing the report's premise was false for this repository (the checker reads thresholds as numbers and refuses `1%`), asked the lead which checker had cleared the stage, fixed the lead's three findings (`d6c1619`), and stopped at the review limit with one finding standing — the blamed code is in another repository and cannot be checked here — "not cleared", the ending the team chose. Two rough edges: the Responder ran the playbook's own exported skill (`.claude/skills/incident/SKILL.md`) as its first action, looked for a way to start a Spex session, and reported falsely that no review round ran (W-34); the Captain's closing reply dropped the record path and the intent id the playbook reported (W-32).

## What they found

- A deploy-stage playbook assumes artifacts (manifests, a deploy script) that a fresh project lacks; the question it asked was the right one, and the owner's answer is where the course should teach "what the procedure needs to exist first".
- W-12, W-17, W-18, W-21 as elsewhere.
